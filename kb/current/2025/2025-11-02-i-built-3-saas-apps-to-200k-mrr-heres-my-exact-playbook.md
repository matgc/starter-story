# I Built 3 SaaS Apps to $200K MRR: Here's My Exact Playbook

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=67zh8_yiPh4)

## Marketing strategy summary

- Target proven B2B SaaS categories where customers already pay but incumbents have weak UX or excessive pricing; Mike's portfolio covers social media aggregation, customer feedback, digital signage, no-code product tours, and group e-cards.
- Reduce idea risk before building: choose a product category that already works, study which competitor features customers value most, and differentiate through better design and usability rather than an unvalidated concept.
- Acquire the first paid users with a private lifetime deal promoted in Reddit communities, private Facebook groups, X, and specialist lifetime-deal groups; Thrill raised about $30,000 this way.
- Charge every early user so they have an incentive to use the product and report what is wrong, then expand reach through AppSumo and a final, higher-priced lifetime deal with a firm closing deadline.
- Turn lifetime-deal cash into durable distribution by publishing landing pages, blog posts, competitor pages, and alternative-to pages early, then ask customers for honest Trustpilot or G2 reviews and answer relevant Reddit questions authentically.
- Use the lifetime-deal phase to target roughly $100,000 of runway while recurring revenue grows; each company starts with four equal co-founders, waits until about $10,000 MRR covers costs, and then distributes profits rather than pursuing a large exit.
- The portfolio reportedly makes just over $200,000 MRR while staying bootstrapped and lean, but the transcript gives no revenue split, profit figure, or proof that every future launch will succeed; it also avoids AI-dependent products because external APIs create platform risk.

### Reusable playbook

1. Choose an existing SaaS category where demand is proven and competitors succeed despite poor UX, high prices, or both.
2. Identify the few competitor features customers value most, then define the smallest useful MVP around them.
3. Assemble a small, product-focused founding team with front-end, back-end, design, and commercial ownership.
4. Launch a paid lifetime deal; do not give accounts away, because paying users are more likely to use the product and provide useful criticism.
5. Sell the private deal directly in relevant Reddit, Facebook, X, and lifetime-deal communities until there is both user feedback and initial capital.
6. Invest that capital immediately in search-focused landing pages, blog posts, competitor comparisons, and alternative pages.
7. Expand the lifetime deal through AppSumo or a similar marketplace to gain more users and target enough cash for one to two years of content production.
8. Run one final, higher-priced lifetime deal for the mailing list, use real scarcity, and then close lifetime access permanently.
9. Ask active customers for honest reviews on Trustpilot, G2, or another relevant review platform to build trust and domain authority.
10. Transition to subscriptions while answering relevant Reddit questions honestly; keep costs lean until MRR replaces the shrinking lifetime-deal runway.
