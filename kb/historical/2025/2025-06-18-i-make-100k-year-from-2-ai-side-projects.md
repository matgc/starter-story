# I Make $100K/Year From 2 AI Side Projects

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CoqAxV6b6j4)

## Marketing strategy summary

- Start with a paid manual task that generative AI can materially improve or replace. Question.ai targeted large technology and publishing companies that spend time creating quizzes, while the founders anticipated that automated quiz generation from text would become viable as AI improved.
- Build distribution before the product. Ramsri wrote blogs about quiz generation, made YouTube videos, and posted every day on LinkedIn about his work; this founder-led content supplied the first users and even helped him find co-founders.
- Build in public consistently rather than chasing individual post performance. His posts ranged from about 10 to 400 likes, but repeated sharing around Question.ai and Supermeme.ai created an initial group of users who could test and champion the products.
- Use those early users as the validation and launch loop: put an initial MVP in their hands, iterate from their response, then ask them to support a Product Hunt launch. Supermeme's existing champions and the inherent sharing appeal of an AI meme generator helped it rank number one on launch day.
- The two products were reported at a combined $8,500 per month, or a little over $100,000 ARR, with lifetime revenue of about $130,000 for Supermeme and $100,000 for Question.ai. They operated at a reported 70-75% profit margin because the founders had no employees and APIs were the largest cost.
- Caveat: large free-user totals did not translate directly into paying customers. The automatic captions report 1.5 million Supermeme users and 150,000 Question.ai users, but only 400 paying Supermeme customers and an unclear captioned range of "18 to 200" for Question.ai.

### Reusable playbook

1. List manual tasks businesses already pay for, then select one that current or near-term AI can make substantially faster, cheaper, or easier.
2. Before building, publish useful niche material through search-oriented blog posts, demonstration videos, and daily founder updates on one social platform.
3. Ship the smallest usable prototype to people attracted by that content and use their reactions to revise the MVP.
4. Keep sharing the build despite uneven engagement, turning early users into champions and using public work to attract possible collaborators.
5. Launch to a concentrated discovery channel such as Product Hunt only after champions exist, then pair their support with a product feature that encourages sharing.
6. Protect side-project economics with a founder-only team, lightweight tools, and regular monitoring of API costs, revenue, conversion, and margins.
