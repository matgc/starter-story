# How I Built It: $20K/Month AI App as a Non-Technical Founder

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64)

## Marketing strategy summary

- Target job seekers who need expert resume guidance but cannot afford a $300-$400 manual review. Sheets Resume turns Colin's recruiting experience and resume rules into an AI service that can review far more resumes at a lower price.
- Validate the problem by helping people manually before building: a detailed Reddit reply earned about 500 upvotes, the resulting resume guide became a top post in the jobs subreddit and the first Google result for "resume advice Reddit," and Colin received about 20,000 questions over six years.
- Confirm willingness to pay with a service that does not scale. Colin and Nate sold manual reviews for a few hundred dollars, raised the price from about $200 to $300 and then $400 as demand exceeded their time, and used that demand to justify an automated version.
- Launch free into an audience already built around useful content. They added the builder to Colin's established Reddit post, created thousands of resumes in the first week, and relied on the product experience and personal help to trigger word of mouth before turning on pricing.
- Keep helpful content, not promotion, at the center of organic acquisition. Colin published Reddit posts about resumes, job-hunt questions, interviews, and salary negotiation to benefit from Reddit's search visibility, mentioning the product without making it the main call to action so readers would trust the advice.
- Extend distribution with user advocacy, refunds or free access that create positive one-to-one experiences, rudimentary Google Ads used to train its algorithm, and an affiliate marketing agency intended to scale awareness without Colin's time.
- Monetize with $99 lifetime access or $29 for one week rather than a recurring subscription. The transcript reports a first $1,000 day within a week, about $20,000 in the second month elsewhere, and roughly 90% net margins; caveats are that one caption says $220,000 in second-month sales, which conflicts with the video's repeated $20,000 claim, and Colin expects today's cheap OpenAI credits to become more expensive.

### Reusable playbook

1. Choose a problem where you have unusual expertise and repeatedly help the target customer for free in a public, searchable community.
2. Track response signals such as upvotes, questions, repeat requests, and search rankings, then sell the work manually to prove that customers will pay.
3. Record the rules behind the manual service and use them to scope an automated product with a trusted technical partner.
4. Release the first version free to the audience around your strongest evergreen content and measure use, outcomes, and recommendations.
5. Turn on simple pricing after usage and word of mouth are proven, matching the billing period to the customer's real need rather than forcing a subscription.
6. Publish more problem-solving content around adjacent high-intent searches, disclose the product lightly, and preserve trust by keeping the advice useful on its own.
7. Add paid search and affiliates only after the organic message works, while monitoring AI costs, margins, refunds, and conflicting revenue claims.
