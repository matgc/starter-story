# The $1.8M Solopreneur Playbook

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vedkm3ZmHIE)

## Marketing strategy summary

- Target startups and other businesses that need high-impact website and brand design but want a faster, simpler alternative to hiring a $200,000-per-year designer, paying a $30,000-$40,000 agency project fee, or searching marketplaces for uncertain talent.
- Validate with speed and an intentionally underpriced offer: Brett built a simple landing page in 12 hours for $29, offered unlimited design for $449 per month, and gained his first paying client within 24 hours. He used the early work to build skill and proof, then raised the monthly price as demand grew from $449 to $1,000, $3,000, and $5,000; the automatic captions render the last stated price as $88,000 per month.
- Productize delivery around firm boundaries: customers buy directly on the website, submit written requests asynchronously through Trello, keep only one active request at a time, and have no calls or meetings. The constraints improve briefs, reduce request volume, and protect fulfillment time.
- Move toward businesses for which a $5,000 monthly invoice is minor and whose alternative costs make the subscription feel inexpensive. The offer combines a proven designer, roughly 48-hour turnaround, one-click purchase, and cancellation at any time.
- Choose work that is both high demand and low touch. The transcript argues that website and branding work has high business impact, can be completed quickly with experience, and usually does not need constant maintenance; it warns that bookkeeping or video editing may be more time-consuming and less profitable under the same model.
- Extend proven expertise into products that do not require more client hours. Brett reportedly spent six hours creating the Scribbles template collection, which was downloaded more than 25,000 times, then created the higher-priced Productize Yourself course; these information products made up 29% of his income.
- Economics and caveats: the transcript says 16 clients at $5,000 per month is about $1 million per year, with zero employees, nearly no cash expenses, and roughly six working hours per day. It separately describes the business as earning $1.8 million, nearly $2 million, and over $2 million, without reconciling those figures or stating profit. It also does not identify a repeatable traffic or outreach channel behind customer acquisition beyond the website, low initial price, reputation, and rising demand.

### Reusable playbook

1. Pick a service with urgent business value, expensive existing alternatives, and output that an experienced specialist can deliver quickly without ongoing maintenance.
2. Launch a minimal purchase page quickly, price the first version low enough to remove buying friction, and use the first paid jobs to validate demand and build skill rather than waiting for premium customers.
3. Track demand and raise the price in stages as proof, speed, and reputation improve; focus the offer on clients whose alternative costs make the premium subscription an easy decision.
4. Replace calls and open-ended access with asynchronous written requests, one active request per client, a fixed delivery rhythm, and clear rules that prevent midstream scope changes.
5. Keep fulfillment solo only while client count and working hours remain sustainable, and preserve margins by avoiding service categories whose recurring workload grows too quickly.
6. Turn knowledge and assets produced through repeated client work into a small downloadable product, validate purchases, and then package the broader operating method into a higher-priced educational product.
7. Add and measure an explicit acquisition channel, because this transcript explains the offer and fulfillment model but does not reveal a repeatable source of prospects.
